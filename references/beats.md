# The beat engine

A beat is the smallest unit of story: one idea, 1–3 sentences, one visible change on screen. Scripts are lists of beats with stable ids (`b0`, `b1`, …) so the narration, the anchors and the timeline can always be matched.

## Explainer arc (how something works)

| Beat | Job | Example line |
|---|---|---|
| **b0 Hook / first event** | Show the surprising state or the result before the cause. | "The knob turns, but the door stays shut." |
| **b1 Failure** | Show what blocks it and the visible consequence. | "While the notches point in different directions, the bar has nowhere to go. It stays locked." |
| **b2 Fix** | Install the solution on screen, with real support (a part that holds, a path that opens). | "Now the notches line up. The bar drops into the gap." |
| **b3 Retest** | Repeat the *same* test in a comparable frame; now it works. | "With the bar seated, the bolt retracts and the door opens." |
| **b4 Limits + close** | Say what the model simplifies; end on the image that sums it up. | "The discs are spread apart for clarity. This is how one position allows one movement." |

Variants:
- **Flow** (water, energy, signal): source → transformation → path → destination → recap of the whole path in one sentence.
- **Balance** (counterweight, lever): what moves → what helps → what the helper does *not* do (kills a misconception) → reversal.

## True-story arc (survival, history, mystery)

| Beat | Job |
|---|---|
| **Cold open** | Start in the middle of the worst moment. Withhold the cause; end the opening on the impossible fact ("…from three thousand meters. Without a parachute."). |
| **Setup** | Who, where, what they loved. Plant the knowledge or object that will save them. |
| **Rising danger** | Warning signs, ordinary routine, the decision that goes wrong. Crescendo. |
| **Turn** | The event itself. Slow it down; explain the physics or the cause simply. |
| **Struggle** | Alternate effort (long shots) with threats (short shots). Each cycle heavier. |
| **Rescue / answer** | Accelerate. Warm light. Pay off what was planted. |
| **Echo** | Aftermath, a related case, a question to the audience, a final line that can be repeated. |

## Hooks that work

- **Impossible fact**: a number or outcome that should not be true. "She fell 3,000 m and walked out of the jungle."
- **Result before cause**: show the end state, ask how. "The door is open. Nobody touched the code."
- **Wrong intuition**: state what everyone assumes, then break it. "The motor doesn't lift the whole elevator."
- **In medias res**: the character already in trouble, details piling up, cause revealed last.

Rules: the hook's picture is the strongest frame of the video; no logo, no greeting, no "in this video". The line under 12 words where possible.

## Open loops

A loop is a question the viewer carries forward. Open one in the hook, at least one more per long-form block, and close each explicitly:
- Plant: "Keep this in mind: in the jungle, water leads to people."
- Pay off: "Remember what I asked you to keep in mind?"

Track loops in a table (planted in → paid off in). An unpaid loop is a defect; a payoff without a plant is a cheat.

## Sentence rules

- One action per sentence; the verb is what we see.
- Concrete nouns the picture can show ("the counterweight", not "the system").
- Numbers spoken the way people say them, and shown once as on-screen text.
- Conversational presenter voice for long-form ("imagine", "and here's the thing"), calm and confident for explainers.
- Cut adjectives the picture already shows.
- Personal reactions are allowed sparingly ("I honestly can't imagine that fear") and need a held close-up plus a 1 s pause.
