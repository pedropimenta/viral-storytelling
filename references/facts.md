# Facts and limits

Viral does not mean loose. A video that gets a fact wrong loses the comments section.

## Rules

1. Research before writing narration. Prefer primary sources: official investigation reports, manufacturers, original research, the person's own memoir or interviews, contemporary newspapers.
2. One claim per row in `templates/facts.md`: claim as spoken → source with direct URL → strength (strong/medium/weak) → limit on screen.
3. Weak or single-source claims are rewritten as hedged ("reports say…") or dropped.
4. When a popular version of the story is wrong, do not repeat it. Note it under "Myths we did not use" and, if useful, correct it on air.
5. When sources disagree (a record, an altitude, a seat number), say so in one line and show an asterisk on screen.
6. Describe the specific model represented ("a drip coffee maker with boil-driven lift"), never generalize to every machine of the type.
7. List fiction, jokes and dramatized details separately so nobody reads them as fact.
8. Never invent quotes, testimonials, audience numbers or results.

## Limits of representation

Close explainers by naming what the animation leaves out (safety parts, controls, real proportions). Close true stories with "Reconstruction — AI-generated images" or the equivalent, and keep sources in the description.
