# Long form (8–12 min, horizontal)

## Block map

About 1,500–1,700 words for 10 minutes at a conversational pace (~150–160 words/min).

| Block | Time | Job | Rhythm |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 Cold open | 0:00–0:25 | Middle of the worst moment; details pile up; the impossible fact lands last, on a hard cut. | Staccato: dry cuts, tight shots, no music until the hit. |
| 1 Channel beat | 0:25–0:40 | One welcome line, "sources in the description", optional subscribe ask. ≤ 15 s. | Short legato; breathing room after the hook. |
| 2 Setup | ~1:30 | Who, where, the world they loved. **Plant** the knowledge/object that pays off later ("keep this in mind…"). | Warm legato; memories can switch look (e.g. home-movie film). |
| 3 Rising danger | ~1:30 | Ordinary routine, warning signs, the decision that goes wrong. | Crescendo: long still shots, then cuts speed up; silence on the last line before the turn. |
| 4 Turn | ~1:00 | The event; slow it down; a clean animated explainer of the cause/physics gives the viewer solid ground. **Mid-video CTA** at the end of this block ("it gets even more unbelievable from here"). | Ritardando: slow motion, then wide shots. |
| 5–7 Struggle | ~3:00 | Alternate effort and threats; each day/cycle heavier. **Pay off** the plant. A held close-up for any personal reaction. | Alternating: walking legato × danger staccato; shots get longer as the body tires. |
| 8 Rescue / answer | ~1:00 | Strangers, help, the reveal of the answer. | Accelerando: cuts speed up, light warms. |
| 9 Aftermath | ~0:45 | What happened next; one human detail. | Heavy, then reflective dissolves. |
| 10 Extra case | ~0:45 | A related story or fact that recontextualizes the main one (check its sources as hard as the main story). | Explainer: clean graphics, one data point at a time. |
| 11 Close | ~0:30 | Echo line the viewer can repeat; a question for the comments; end card. | Resolution: movement rises (detail → sky), music opens, final still frame. |

## Scenes of 10 seconds

For AI-generated or stock-heavy video, cut the timeline into 10 s scenes. Each scene has:
- a **first frame** and a **last frame** (reference images), plus extra frames for hard cuts inside the scene (5 s + 5 s or 4 s + 3 s + 3 s);
- one camera behaviour, one action, one secondary detail, and how it exits into the next scene;
- a type: `REAL` (live-action look), `ANIM` (clean graphics), or a memory look.

Use `templates/storyboard-scene.md`. 80 scenes × 10 s ≈ 13 min of picture; the narration decides which scenes are trimmed.

## Presenter voice

- Opens in the action, not with a greeting.
- Talks to one person: "imagine", "you know why?", "remember what I asked you to keep?".
- Explains causes in one plain sentence ("Normally pilots go around storms. This flight went straight in.").
- Corrects popular myths with sources, and says when a fact is disputed.

## Look and camera for true stories

Documentary reconstruction reads as more truthful than glossy cinema:
- handheld at eye level, one step behind or beside the subject; the camera searches, reframes, loses and finds focus;
- grainy film, ungraded colour, available light, dirt or rain on the lens;
- quick manual zooms to find details instead of smooth push-ins;
- rig-mounted cameras inside vehicles; POV shots sparingly;
- strong shake only in panic moments; calm scenes breathe but stay stable (10 minutes of shake is tiring);
- animated explainers stay clean — they are the breathing space.

## Continuity bible

Write it before generating anything and paste it into every image/video prompt:
- the character's fixed marks (injury side and intensity, clothes, props, hand size/age);
- screen direction (the vehicle always travels left → right; progress = moving right; lost = facing camera or left);
- which props appear where (plant/payoff table);
- a reference image of the approved look for each character, and which references get rejected by content filters (use milder ones).
