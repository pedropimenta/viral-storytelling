# Short form (45–100 s, vertical)

## Shape

| Time | Beat | Picture |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 s | Hook | Strongest frame, already moving. Close or medium shot of the key part/person. |
| 3–15 s | First event | Establish what we are looking at (cutaway, section view, location). |
| 15–40 s | Failure | Problem and its consequence, in close-up on the anchor word. |
| 40–65 s | Fix | The solution installed, with visible support. |
| 65–85 s | Retest | Same test, same framing as the failure, now resolved. |
| 85–100 s | Limits + close | Wide shot of the whole thing; one sentence recap; loop back to the first frame if possible. |

For a 45–60 s short, merge first event into the hook and keep failure → fix → retest.

## Rhythm

- ~160–170 words per minute; about 250 words for 100 s.
- A visual change at least every 2–3 s (cut, zoom, part moving, text appearing).
- Camera travels continuously to the part named in the line; the action happens on the word, in close-up.
- Subtitles at the bottom, always rendered in the scene. Other text: short, one at a time.
- Music under the voice, never over it; silence is allowed before the turn.

## Loop ending

End on a frame that matches or echoes the first frame so the replay feels continuous. The last line can answer the hook's question in the fewest words possible.

## Short version of a long story

When cutting a long true story into a short:
1. Keep only the cold open, the turn and the answer.
2. Hook with the impossible fact in the first line.
3. Use the 3–4 strongest shots, each 5–10 s, re-timed to the new narration.
4. End with one sentence that sends people to the long version or to a question in the comments.

## Example (cut-away coffee maker, 5 beats)

```
b0  "The water starts cold in this {start}reservoir. Even so, it has to reach the top to pass through the coffee."
b1  "First, the {reservoir}reservoir feeds the lower tube. The {heater}heater warms the water. {bubbles}Bubbles of steam push slugs of water up the {rise}rising tube."
b2  "At the {shower}spreader, water falls over the grounds. The dark grains stay in the {filter}filter while the liquid passes through."
b3  "The {pot}pot collects the coffee by gravity: cold water, heat below, up the tube, coffee falling down."
b4  "Real machines have controls and safety parts this model leaves out. {end}"
```
