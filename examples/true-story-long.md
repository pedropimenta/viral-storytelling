# Example — long-form true story: the girl who fell from the sky (~10 min, 16:9)

Public story: in December 1971 a 17-year-old passenger survived the break-up of an airliner over the Peruvian rainforest and walked for 11 days until she found help. (Check every detail in primary sources: her memoir, the investigation, contemporary press.)

**One sentence:** she survived because of what her parents taught her about the jungle — find water and follow it.
**Hook type:** in medias res + impossible fact.

## Continuity bible (excerpt)
- Right eye swollen and half-closed, moderate — the same in every jungle scene.
- Leaf bandage only on the right arm; one sandal on the right foot; thin teenage hands, never adult hands.
- The plane always flies left → right; in the jungle she moves right when progressing, faces camera or left when lost.
- Plants and payoffs: notebook with the river (setup → struggle), wristwatch (flight → waking), sandal (flight → day 1).

## Look
Handheld 16 mm documentary reconstruction for real scenes; home-movie film for memories; clean navy-and-amber animation for explanations. Strong shake only in panic moments. Injuries implied, never shown.

## Myths not used
Details repeated online but unsupported by the sources (burn degrees, an exact seat number, lightning "on the engine" instead of the wing) were left out.
