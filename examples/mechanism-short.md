# Example — explainer short: the counterweight elevator (~100 s, vertical)

**One sentence:** the motor only overcomes the *difference* between the car and the counterweight.
**Hook type:** wrong intuition.

```
b0 hook        "This {start}car is going up. But the motor isn't fighting all of that weight on its own.
                On the other side of the cables, a mass travels the opposite way."
b1 setup       "At the top, the {pulley}drive pulley grips the cables by friction. When it turns, the {car}car
                rises on one side while the {counterweight}counterweight drops on the other."
b2 key idea    "That heavy block {balance}balances the car and part of the load. The machine only has to beat
                the difference between the two sides. The counterweight doesn't replace the motor."
b3 retest      "{guides}Guide rails keep both on vertical paths. At the stop, the {door}car meets the floor,
                and only then do the doors {open}open."
b4 close       "When the motor {reverse}reverses, everything swaps: the car goes down, the block goes up.
                Two paths tied by the same cables. {end}"
```

| Anchor | Shows | Zoom | Text |
|---|---|---|---|
| start | car rising in a cutaway shaft | whole shaft | none |
| pulley | pulley turning, cables moving | pulley groove | "drive pulley" |
| counterweight | block sliding down as car rises | block | "counterweight" |
| balance | scale icon over both sides, arrows equal | split frame | "difference" |
| open | doors open only after car aligns with floor | door edge | none |
| reverse | motion reverses, same framing as start | whole shaft | none |

Why it works: the hook breaks an intuition, the middle kills a misconception ("doesn't replace the motor"), the close repeats the opening frame in reverse — a natural loop.
